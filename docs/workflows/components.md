# Components

For details on what components are, please see the component section of the [repository file structure document](RepositoryFileStructure.md).

COHO's components are all generated from [ROBOT templates](http://robot.obolibrary.org/template), the tables in `src/templates`. They are listed under `components` in `owlmake.yaml`:

```
components:
  products:
  - filename: GWAS.owl
    use_template: true
    template_options: '--prefix "COHO: http://www.ebi.ac.uk/coho/COHO_" ...'
    templates:
    - GWAS.csv
```

`template_options` gives the prefixes the templates' CURIEs are written with (`COHO:`, `dbpedia:`, `RO:`, `oio:`).

## Editing a component

Never edit the files in `src/ontology/components`: they are rebuilt from the templates. Instead:

1. Edit the template in `src/templates` (for example `src/templates/GWAS.csv`)
2. Rebuild the components:
   ```
   om make recreate-components
   ```
3. Run the [quality control checks](QualityControl.md): `om make test IMP=false`
4. Commit the template and the rebuilt component together

## Curation files

`src/templates` holds only ROBOT templates. The evidence and curatorial decisions the generated templates are built from live in `src/curation`: an `_evidence.tsv` per topic (the reviewed evidence, one row per cohort) and a `_curated.tsv` (hand overrides, which win). Edit the curated files, never the generated templates. All four scripts skip individuals that `coho-edit.owl` marks `owl:deprecated`, so obsoleting a term withdraws its generated assertions on the next run; its evidence rows stay as the record of what was found.

The source tables the reviews were made against are there too: `ega_studies.tsv` and `ega_study_cohorts.tsv` (EGA), `gwas_catalog_cohorts.tsv.gz` (GWAS Catalog), and `new_cohorts_ega.tsv` (the cohort names that came up in the EGA review and were not in COHO, with any consortium or parent cohort noted; `new_cohorts_ega_check.tsv` is their match against the list COHO was seeded from). Those names were worked through on 2026-09-22: `new_cohorts_ega_decisions.tsv` records, for every name on that list and every verified EGA match that no COHO term covered, whether it was minted as a new cohort (with its ID and label), resolved to an existing term, or not added and why. The minted cohorts' examples, definitions, locations and memberships were entered through the curated files, each row noting the EGA study it rests on. Refreshing a source means replacing its table and re-running the scripts. A second review, of 2026-09-23, worked through the cohort tags the GWAS Catalog and the PGS Catalog use most: `pgs_catalog_cohorts.csv` is the PGS Catalog's cohort list, `catalog_cohort_gaps.tsv` lists the PGS cohort names and GWAS Catalog cohort tags that no COHO term covered, in tiers by the number of GWAS Catalog studies that use them, and `catalog_cohorts_review.tsv` records the decision on each: minted as a cohort or an aggregation (with label, synonyms, type, acronym clashes, example study, definition and its drafter, location and parent, each with its source), resolved to an existing term, merged into another row as a duplicate spelling (`same as`), or not a cohort. Its `COHO ID` column records the term each row was minted as or resolved to; the examples, definitions, locations and memberships of the 108 cohorts minted from it went into the curated files, and their sub-cohort links and acronym editor notes into `coho-edit.owl`. After minting, each of those 108 terms was checked by independent reviewers in the same way as the second tier's (`catalog_cohorts_tier_a_check.csv`, with the same columns), and its `applied` column records what was done with each on 2026-09-23: 26 definitions were rewritten by the check and 28 given corrected sources, 57 names and 100 memberships were added, six examples or their evidence and six locations were corrected, and the Amish Complex Disease Research Program became a cohort aggregation. The review's second tier, 167 less used names, was decided and minted on the same basis on 2026-09-23: 142 new cohorts and aggregations (COHO:0000474 to COHO:0000615), among them the parent cohorts and consortia the sources named for them, with the country names their locations needed added to `src/templates/gaz_xrefs.tsv`. After minting, each of those 142 terms was checked by independent reviewers against the cohort's own sources: what its tags denote in the papers that use them, its definition claim by claim, its names, duplicates in COHO, its type, parent and members. `catalog_cohorts_tier_b_check.csv` has a row per term with its status, a verdict on its definition and each correction with its source and quote, and its `applied` column records what was done with each on 2026-09-23: 34 definitions were rewritten by the check, names and memberships were added, two terms were relabelled, KARE (COHO:0000529) was obsoleted as another name for the Ansan and Ansung Study (COHO:0000530), and the few corrections not applied say why. The third tier, 374 names, was drafted the same way and then, before anything was minted, checked row by row by independent reviewers, who tested what each tag denotes in the papers that use it, the cohort's own name, each claim of the definition against the cohort's sources, duplicates already in COHO, the type and the parent. Their corrections were applied to the review, and each corrected row's note ends with what the check changed ("Verified 2026-09-23: …"). The tier was minted on 2026-09-23: 330 new cohorts and aggregations (COHO:0000616 to COHO:0000945), including the two umbrella terms the check called for (the COPSAC cohorts and the ProHealth Kaiser GWAS). Its changes to terms already in COHO (the Lothian Birth Cohorts becoming the aggregation of their 1921 and 1936 cohorts, the TRAILS definition widened to both of its cohorts, memberships, acronyms, and the relabellings the check proposed and the curator accepted) are listed with their evidence in `catalog_cohorts_review_existing_terms.tsv`. The fourth tier, 597 names each used by one to four GWAS Catalog studies, is being worked through in a way that scales. A script matches every name against COHO's labels and synonyms and groups the names that are one cohort. A second gathers each name's evidence once, in bulk and through the EBI network: the abstracts, full texts and supplementary files of its GWAS Catalog papers (Europe PMC first, open-access text before anything else), and papers found by searching Europe PMC for the cohort's name, keeping the numbered sentences that name the cohort. A model drafts each term from those sentences alone, citing them by number, so that every quote is verbatim, and an independent reviewer checks each draft against the cohort's own sources and records its corrections as structured changes. A pilot of 50 names chosen at random was minted this way on 2026-09-24 (COHO:0000946 to COHO:0000988, 35 cohorts and 8 aggregations; three names resolved to existing terms and four are not cohorts), and the other 547 names the same day (COHO:0000989 to COHO:0001450, 462 terms, among them 15 further cohorts that a name's tag or PGS Catalog id turned out to denote; 52 names resolved to existing terms, 39 are not cohorts, 8 are spellings of another name, one waits on a name of the fifth tier). Their review rows' notes begin "Tier D (D…)", the name's key in the tier, and record what the check changed ("Checked 2026-09-24: …"). Parents that are names of the fifth tier are recorded in the note ("Parent left for later: …") and linked when that tier is minted. Many of the new aggregations have no members yet: the checks name candidate members in their notes. The fifth tier, 1,250 PGS Catalog cohort names that no GWAS Catalog study uses, goes through the same pipeline, its evidence being the papers the PGS Catalog records for each name and papers found by name, and its example a paper that used the cohort. The 220 names of the 22 batches whose checks had finished when the run paused were minted on 2026-09-24, except five whose parent or twin is a fifth-tier name not yet checked, which wait for it: 164 new terms (COHO:0001451 to COHO:0001614, 150 cohorts and 14 aggregations, among them three further cohorts that a name's PGS Catalog id turned out to denote); 28 names resolved to existing terms, 15 are not cohorts, 6 are spellings of another name and 5 are unresolved. Their review rows' notes begin "Tier E (E…)"; two rows beginning "Tier E. Late link" join fourth-tier cohorts to their new fifth-tier aggregations. The checks of the fourth and fifth tiers also found faults in terms already in COHO, which were fixed on 2026-09-24 and are recorded with their evidence in `catalog_cohorts_review_existing_terms.tsv`: the Cardio-metabolic Genome Epidemiology Network (COHO:0000027) became the aggregation of its member studies, COHO:0000346 was identified as the British Heart Foundation Family Heart Study and relabelled, the AA-DHS names and example moved from the Diabetes Heart Study to their own term, GENIE became a sub-cohort of H-PEACE, the Caerphilly Prospective Study took the GWAS Catalog's CaPS studies, and the definitions of the GABRIEL Advanced Surveys, the GenomeAsia 100K project and the Australian Alcohol and Nicotine Studies were corrected. `src/scripts/mint_cohorts.py` mints the review's decided rows from a given row on: the new terms' names, types, sub-cohort links and editor notes for shared names in `coho-edit.owl`, their definitions, examples, locations and memberships in the curated files, and the new COHO IDs in the review.

## Example studies

Each cohort is linked to a study that used it, as an `example of usage` (IAO:0000112) annotation holding the study's accession (an EGA `EGAS…`, GWAS Catalog `GCST…`, MetaboLights `MTBLS…` or PRIDE `PXD…`, or the paper's `PMID:` where no archive study is given). A cohort in a subset needs an example from that subset's resource, which the `subset-example` QC check enforces, so a cohort in two subsets has two examples and one in none has one. Each example carries the study's publication as a `PMID:` xref on the annotation and a title as `dcterms:title` on it (the paper's, or the EGA study's own where it has no paper), which is what OLS shows beside the accession. Where a reviewer's evidence that the study used the cohort was verified against its source, the quote and the source URL are also on the annotation, as `rdfs:comment` and a second `oboInOwl:hasDbXref`. These come from the `example_studies.owl` component, whose template, `src/templates/example_studies.tsv`, is itself generated and should not be edited by hand:

```
src/scripts/example_studies.py
```

Paper titles are looked up in Europe PMC and kept in `src/curation/example_studies_titles.tsv`. It reads, from `src/curation`, the EGA studies a human reviewed for cohort mentions (`ega_studies.tsv`, with their verified matches in `ega_study_cohorts.tsv`) and the GWAS Catalog's studies with one cohort per line (`gwas_catalog_cohorts.tsv.gz`, a Catalog export of 2026-09). How a study is chosen is described at the top of the script.

Cohorts the script finds no study for are printed, some with a candidate study that matched on an acronym alone and needs a curator to confirm it, and so are subset members that lack an example from their subset's resource, with the studies the tables suggest. To give a cohort an example by hand, or to overrule the script's choice, add a line to `src/curation/example_studies_curated.tsv` (tab-separated columns `ID`, `example study`, `PMID`, `note`, and optionally `quote` and `URL`, the verbatim evidence that the study used the cohort and where it was read) and run the script again. There is one line per example: a cohort with curated lines gets exactly those examples and no automatic one, so a cohort that needs a second example has both written there. A line with a PMID and no accession gives the paper itself as the example. A line with both left empty rejects the script's choice without replacing it: acronyms are shared between cohorts (the GWAS Catalog's `ACP` on GCST90281184 is an Amish cohort, not the Asian Cancer Project), so a match on one is not always right. The current curated rows come from two reviews of every cohort on 2026-09-22, merged into `src/curation/example_studies_evidence.tsv` (one row per cohort: verdict, accession, PMID, evidence quote, source URL, whether the quote was verified, confidence, and the reviewer's remark); accessions, PMIDs and quotes were checked against Europe PMC, PMC, the GWAS Catalog and EGA before being accepted.

## Data collection locations

Where a cohort's participants were recruited is asserted with `has_data_collection_location`, to a country or region from the `gaz_xrefs.owl` component, by the `locations.owl` component. Its template, `src/templates/locations.tsv`, is generated by `src/scripts/locations.py` from `src/curation/location_evidence.tsv` (a review of every assertion, one row per cohort with the countries asserted, a verdict — `correct`, `incomplete`, `wrong`, `unverified`, or `unlocated` for a cohort COHO had not placed — the right countries, the evidence and whether its quote was verified; an `unlocated` row with no countries records why none could be given) and `src/curation/location_curated.tsv` (overrides: `ID`, `countries` as pipe-separated dbpedia resource names, `quote`, `URL`, `note`; an empty `countries` removes the cohort's locations, and a cohort may have several rows, one per quote, each attaching its quote to its own countries). Where the evidence quote was verified in its source, it goes on the assertion as `rdfs:comment`, with the source URL as `oboInOwl:hasDbXref`. The location is where participants were recruited, not where the data were analysed or are held. A country the review needs that `gaz_xrefs.tsv` lacks must be added there first, with its GAZ xref and continent; the script refuses to run otherwise. The script lists the individuals in `coho-edit.owl` that have no location, so the gaps are visible after each run.

## Definitions

Each cohort's textual definition (`IAO:0000115`) comes from the `definitions.owl` component, generated by `src/scripts/definitions.py` from `src/curation/definitions_evidence.tsv` (one row per cohort: the definition, its source, a supporting quote and whether the quote was found in the source) and `src/curation/definitions_curated.tsv` (overrides; an empty definition withholds one). The cohorts to define are the individuals declared in `coho-edit.owl`, so a newly minted cohort is reported as undefined until a row is added for it. Each definition carries its source as `IAO:0000119` (the paper's PMID, or the URL of the cohort's own site; a definition drawn from several sources lists them separated by `|` in the curated file and carries one `IAO:0000119` for each) and, as `dcterms:contributor`, the agents that drafted and checked it: the definitions were written by an AI model from sources it retrieved and checked by another against those sources, and the annotations say so on every one. A definition supplied through the curated file carries only its drafter's annotation, taken from the file's optional `drafted by` column (for example "Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), 2026-09" for the cohorts minted from the GWAS and PGS Catalog review); where that column is empty, the definition was written by the checking model alone and names it. The curated definitions have no `checked by` contributor. Those of the catalog review's third, fourth and fifth tiers were checked against the cohorts' sources by independent reviewers before minting, and those of its second tier after minting (the check rewrote the ones it found wanting), as the curated file's note on each says; the rest have not been checked by a second model or person. `src/curation/type_changes.tsv` lists the cohorts whose type (cohort or cohort aggregation) the drafting suggested changing, and which were applied.

## Aggregation members

A cohort aggregation is linked to each cohort it combines, and to each aggregation it includes, by `hasCohort`, from the `aggregation_members.owl` component generated by `src/scripts/aggregation_members.py` from `src/curation/aggregation_members_evidence.tsv` (one row per aggregation and member from a review of every aggregation's membership: the member as its source names it, its COHO ID where it exists or "new", the source, a quote, whether the quote was verified, and per aggregation whether the listed members are cohorts at all — sources that list countries, sites or institutions are marked `include = no`) and `src/curation/aggregation_members_curated.tsv` (overrides; a note beginning "remove" drops a membership). Only members that exist in COHO are asserted; the others are the input to minting new cohorts, after which re-running the script links them. Each assertion carries the quote naming the member as `rdfs:comment` and the source URL as `oboInOwl:hasDbXref`.

## Adding a component

1. Create the template, `src/templates/your-component-name.tsv` (or `.csv`)
2. Add the component to the `components` list in `owlmake.yaml`, as above
3. Bring the repository into step with `owlmake.yaml`:
   ```
   om update-repo
   ```
   This adds the component to the imports of the edit file (`src/ontology/coho-edit.owl`), tells the XML catalog (`src/ontology/catalog-v001.xml`) where its IRI is to be found, so that Protégé and the build can load it, and creates a placeholder `src/ontology/components/your-component-name.owl`.
4. Build it: `om make recreate-components`

Removing a component is the same in reverse: take it out of `owlmake.yaml`, run `om update-repo`, and delete its file.

A component can also be derived from SSSOM mappings (`use_mappings: true` with a list of `mappings`) or fetched from a remote resource (`source: https://example.org/component-source.owl`). One that is generated by a process of its own is described as a target, see [Build configuration](BuildConfiguration.md#building-something-in-a-way-of-your-own).
