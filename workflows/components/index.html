    Components

    For details on what components are, please see the component section of the repository file structure document.

    COHO's components are all generated from ROBOT templates, the tables in src/templates. They are listed under components in owlmake.yaml:

    components:
  products:
  - filename: GWAS.owl
    use_template: true
    template_options: '--prefix "COHO: http://www.ebi.ac.uk/coho/COHO_" ...'
    templates:
    - GWAS.csv

    template_options gives the prefixes the templates' CURIEs are written with (COHO:, dbpedia:, RO:, oio:).

    Editing a component

    Never edit the files in src/ontology/components: they are rebuilt from the templates. Instead:

    1. Edit the template in src/templates (for example src/templates/GWAS.csv)
    2. Rebuild the components: 
      om make recreate-components
    3. Run the quality control checks: om make test IMP=false
    4. Commit the template and the rebuilt component together

    A new component needs three more things: its entry under components in owlmake.yaml, an Import of its IRI in coho-edit.owl, and a line mapping that IRI to the file in src/ontology/catalog-v001.xml; without the last, the merge and the reasoner silently leave it out.

    Curation files

    src/templates holds only ROBOT templates. The evidence and curatorial decisions the generated templates are built from live in src/curation: an _evidence.tsv per topic (the reviewed evidence, one row per cohort) and a _curated.tsv (hand overrides, which win). Edit the curated files, never the generated templates. All four scripts skip individuals that coho-edit.owl marks owl:deprecated, so obsoleting a term withdraws its generated assertions on the next run; its evidence rows stay as the record of what was found.

    The source tables the reviews were made against are there too: ega_studies.tsv and ega_study_cohorts.tsv (EGA), gwas_catalog_cohorts.tsv.gz (GWAS Catalog), and new_cohorts_ega.tsv (the cohort names that came up in the EGA review and were not in COHO, with any consortium or parent cohort noted; new_cohorts_ega_check.tsv is their match against the list COHO was seeded from). Those names were worked through on 2026-09-22: new_cohorts_ega_decisions.tsv records, for every name on that list and every verified EGA match that no COHO term covered, whether it was minted as a new cohort (with its ID and label), resolved to an existing term, or not added and why. The minted cohorts' examples, definitions, locations and memberships were entered through the curated files, each row noting the EGA study it rests on. Refreshing a source means replacing its table and re-running the scripts. A second review, of 2026-09-23, worked through the cohort tags the GWAS Catalog and the PGS Catalog use most: pgs_catalog_cohorts.csv is the PGS Catalog's cohort list, catalog_cohort_gaps.tsv lists the PGS cohort names and GWAS Catalog cohort tags that no COHO term covered, in tiers by the number of GWAS Catalog studies that use them, and catalog_cohorts_review.tsv records the decision on each: minted as a cohort or an aggregation (with label, synonyms, type, acronym clashes, example study, definition and its drafter, location and parent, each with its source), resolved to an existing term, merged into another row as a duplicate spelling (same as), or not a cohort. Its COHO ID column records the term each row was minted as or resolved to; the examples, definitions, locations and memberships of the 108 cohorts minted from it went into the curated files, and their sub-cohort links and acronym editor notes into coho-edit.owl. The review's second tier, 167 less used names, was decided and minted on the same basis on 2026-09-23: 142 new cohorts and aggregations (COHO:0000474 to COHO:0000615), among them the parent cohorts and consortia the sources named for them, with the country names their locations needed added to src/templates/gaz_xrefs.tsv. After minting, each of those 142 terms was checked by independent reviewers against the cohort's own sources: what its tags denote in the papers that use them, its definition claim by claim, its names, duplicates in COHO, its type, parent and members. catalog_cohorts_tier_b_check.csv has a row per term with its status, a verdict on its definition and each correction with its source and quote, and its applied column records what was done with each on 2026-09-23: 34 definitions were rewritten by the check, names and memberships were added, two terms were relabelled, KARE (COHO:0000529) was obsoleted as another name for the Ansan and Ansung Study (COHO:0000530), and the few corrections not applied say why. The fourth tier, 597 names each used by one to four GWAS Catalog studies, is being worked through in a way that scales. A script matches every name against COHO's labels and synonyms and groups the names that are one cohort. A second gathers each name's evidence once, in bulk and through the EBI network: the abstracts, full texts and supplementary files of its GWAS Catalog papers (Europe PMC first, open-access text before anything else), and papers found by searching Europe PMC for the cohort's name, keeping the numbered sentences that name the cohort. A pilot of 50 names chosen at random was minted this way on 2026-09-24 (COHO:0000946 to COHO:0000988, 35 cohorts and 8 aggregations; three names resolved to existing terms and four are not cohorts), and the other 547 names the same day (COHO:0000989 to COHO:0001450, 462 terms, among them 15 further cohorts that a name's tag or PGS Catalog id turned out to denote; 52 names resolved to existing terms, 39 are not cohorts, 8 are spellings of another name, one waits on a name of the fifth tier). Many of the new aggregations have no members yet: the checks name candidate members in their notes. The fifth tier, 1,250 PGS Catalog cohort names that no GWAS Catalog study uses, goes through the same pipeline, its evidence being the papers the PGS Catalog records for each name and papers found by name, and its example a paper that used the cohort. The 220 names of the 22 batches whose checks had finished when the run paused were minted on 2026-09-24, except five whose parent or twin is a fifth-tier name not yet checked, which wait for it: 164 new terms (COHO:0001451 to COHO:0001614, 150 cohorts and 14 aggregations, among them three further cohorts that a name's PGS Catalog id turned out to denote); 28 names resolved to existing terms, 15 are not cohorts, 6 are spellings of another name and 5 are unresolved. The checks of the fourth and fifth tiers also found faults in terms already in COHO, which were fixed on 2026-09-24 and are recorded with their evidence in catalog_cohorts_review_existing_terms.tsv: the Cardio-metabolic Genome Epidemiology Network (COHO:0000027) became the aggregation of its member studies, COHO:0000346 was identified as the British Heart Foundation Family Heart Study and relabelled, the AA-DHS names and example moved from the Diabetes Heart Study to their own term, GENIE became a sub-cohort of H-PEACE, the Caerphilly Prospective Study took the GWAS Catalog's CaPS studies, and the definitions of the GABRIEL Advanced Surveys, the GenomeAsia 100K project and the Australian Alcohol and Nicotine Studies were corrected. Of the 117, 55 were curated as cohorts and 4 as aggregations, 41 obsoleted as not cohorts, 13 merged into existing terms (among them the Rush Memory and Aging Project, KoGES, BioMe, the Hong Kong Diabetes Register and CRCGEN) and 4 left unresolved; the names of the six countries of the Silk Road cohort were added to gaz_xrefs.tsv. On 2026-09-25 the 425 placeholders the PGS Catalog links papers to were given drafted definitions without being curated: their evidence was gathered again from the enlarged cache (evidence.py), rendered in 44 batches of ten (render.py, prefix q), and drafted by one Sonnet agent per batch following DRAFT.md, from the numbered units alone; pipeline/ph_definitions.py then ran the automatic checks on every draft and lifted the definition of each draft whose verdict was a new cohort or aggregation, whose definition cited quotable units and gave no size, into definitions_curated.tsv with the cited papers as its sources, the drafter as its only contributor and a note that it is unchecked. 275 placeholders got a definition this way (one whose draft the size filter had stopped on a cholesterol threshold was added by hand); 131 had evidence too thin to define and 10 looked to the drafter like institutions rather than cohorts, 6 were drafted as duplicates of curated terms and 4 as duplicates of other placeholders, all of which is recorded per name in the pipeline repository (runs/tierD/draft/A_q*.jsonl, q_drafts.tsv) for the check that will curate them. The 669 candidate pairs that survived filtering were each checked against the cached text by an independent reviewer, who had to find a sentence stating that one cohort is part of the other or a member of the aggregation, and every accepted quote was verified against the cached text. src/curation/link_sweep_2026-09-25.csv is the record: one row per pair with the verdict (54 confirmed links, 10 of them sub-cohort links and 44 memberships, with their quote and source; 594 rejected, mostly cohorts merely named together or a synonym that meant something else; 5 unresolved), the reviewer's note, and the batch.     On 2026-09-25 the number of participants of every curated cohort and aggregation was recorded, with its evidence, in src/curation/cohort_participants.csv, one row per term (1,818 rows: 1,585 cohorts and 233 aggregations). Definitions carry no sizes by design, so this table is where a cohort's size lives. The number is the one the cohort itself reports: the people enrolled or recruited, as its design or profile paper or website states it; cases and controls separately for a case-control study; the randomised participants of a trial; for an aggregation, the pooled number of its main analysis, or the number of member studies where only that is reported. It is never computed, summed or inferred, and each rests on a verbatim sentence from a source that was read (quote, with source a PMID or URL); counts says what the number counts and year the year it refers to, confidence how well the source fits, needs the closed papers that could not be read, and note the other numbers seen and why this one was chosen, or why none could be given. The batches (batch) were worked by independent reviewers from the downloaded papers and, where those gave no number, from the cohort's own pages, and every quote was then checked by script against the cached text, Europe PMC or the page: 1,407 rows give a total, 245 give cases and controls without a stated total, and 166 give no number; 1,690 quotes were found verbatim (quote verified), 102 rows carry no quote because they give no number, and 26 quotes come from supplementary spreadsheets, an infographic or page markup that the check cannot read. The numbers are asserted on the terms by the participants.owl component (see Participants below), and coho-cohorts.csv has them as columns.

    Example studies

    Each cohort is linked to a study that used it, as an example of usage (IAO:0000112) annotation holding the study's accession (an EGA EGAS…, GWAS Catalog GCST…, MetaboLights MTBLS…, PRIDE PXD… or PGS Catalog PGS…, or the paper's PMID: where no archive study is given). A cohort in a subset needs an example from that subset's resource, which the subset-example QC check enforces, so a cohort in two subsets has two examples and one in none has one. Each example carries the study's publication as a PMID: xref on the annotation and a title as dcterms:title on it (the paper's, or the EGA study's own where it has no paper), which is what OLS shows beside the accession. Where a reviewer's evidence that the study used the cohort was verified against its source, the quote and the source URL are also on the annotation, as rdfs:comment and a second oboInOwl:hasDbXref. These come from the example_studies.owl component, whose template, src/templates/example_studies.tsv, is itself generated and should not be edited by hand:

    src/scripts/example_studies.py

    Paper titles are looked up in Europe PMC and kept in src/curation/example_studies_titles.tsv. The examples of the PGS subset come from the PGS Catalog: src/scripts/pgs_examples.py reads the Catalog's REST API (every score, performance metric and sample set, cached outside the repository) and gives each term a score (PGS…) that used the cohort, with the PMID and title of the publication in which it did so: a use of the cohort's own data first (a score's training samples, then a sample set a score was evaluated on), then the samples of the GWAS a score's variant associations came from, and among those the sample naming the fewest cohorts. The Catalog's record is the evidence, so the curated line carries no quote and its note says where in the record the cohort is. On 2026-09-25 the 794 placeholders were done this way: 425 were given an example (85 from training samples, 130 from evaluations, 210 from source GWAS), and 369 are cohorts the Catalog's cohort table lists but no score or evaluation uses, which the script prints and which stay without an example. The curated terms of the subset were then done with --all: 49 were given a PGS Catalog example beside the paper or GWAS Catalog example they had, and the 28 the Catalog uses in no score were taken out of the subset, recorded in subset_examples_backfill.tsv, since the subset-example check now covers the PGS subset (a placeholder is exempt and stays; --all after curating placeholders gives each newly curated term its example or takes it out). The whole of what the Catalog records is in src/curation/pgs_catalog_publications.tsv, written by src/scripts/pgs_publications.py for every live term that has a cohort id in the Catalog, not only the subset: one line per term, Catalog id and paper, the paper being a PGS Catalog publication (a score developed from the cohort's data or from its GWAS, or an evaluation of a score on it) or the source GWAS itself, with PMID, DOI, title, the Catalog's publication id and the roles the cohort has in it. The Catalog id of a term minted or resolved by the catalog review is the review's; for the cohorts COHO had before the review, the id is matched by name, exactly, to a term's label or synonym, or to the Catalog's full name for it, and the placed by column says which; an id the review found not to be a cohort is left out, and one that no term carries is printed (on 2026-09-25 two: MYBRCA and NIHR BIORESOURCE). The table then had 11,332 lines for 1,338 terms (425 placeholders, 913 curated) and 1,108 distinct PMIDs, with 188 papers the Catalog has only a DOI for. The curation pipeline's bulk fetch reads the table, so every paper the Catalog links to a term is downloaded in one run, ready for the curation of the placeholders. It reads, from src/curation, the EGA studies a human reviewed for cohort mentions (ega_studies.tsv, with their verified matches in ega_study_cohorts.tsv) and the GWAS Catalog's studies with one cohort per line (gwas_catalog_cohorts.tsv.gz, a Catalog export of 2026-09). How a study is chosen is described at the top of the script.

    Cohorts the script finds no study for are printed, some with a candidate study that matched on an acronym alone and needs a curator to confirm it, and so are subset members that lack an example from their subset's resource, with the studies the tables suggest. To give a cohort an example by hand, or to overrule the script's choice, add a line to src/curation/example_studies_curated.tsv (tab-separated columns ID, example study, PMID, note, and optionally quote and URL, the verbatim evidence that the study used the cohort and where it was read) and run the script again. There is one line per example: a cohort with curated lines gets exactly those examples and no automatic one, so a cohort that needs a second example has both written there. A line with a PMID and no accession gives the paper itself as the example. A line with both left empty rejects the script's choice without replacing it: acronyms are shared between cohorts (the GWAS Catalog's ACP on GCST90281184 is an Amish cohort, not the Asian Cancer Project), so a match on one is not always right. The current curated rows come from two reviews of every cohort on 2026-09-22, merged into src/curation/example_studies_evidence.tsv (one row per cohort: verdict, accession, PMID, evidence quote, source URL, whether the quote was verified, confidence, and the reviewer's remark); accessions, PMIDs and quotes were checked against Europe PMC, PMC, the GWAS Catalog and EGA before being accepted.

    Data collection locations

    Where a cohort's participants were recruited is asserted with has_data_collection_location, to a country or region from the gaz_xrefs.owl component, by the locations.owl component. Its template, src/templates/locations.tsv, is generated by src/scripts/locations.py from src/curation/location_evidence.tsv (a review of every assertion, one row per cohort with the countries asserted, a verdict — correct, incomplete, wrong, unverified, or unlocated for a cohort COHO had not placed — the right countries, the evidence and whether its quote was verified; an unlocated row with no countries records why none could be given) and src/curation/location_curated.tsv (overrides: ID, countries as pipe-separated dbpedia resource names, quote, URL, note; an empty countries removes the cohort's locations, and a cohort may have several rows, one per quote, each attaching its quote to its own countries). Where the evidence quote was verified in its source, it goes on the assertion as rdfs:comment, with the source URL as oboInOwl:hasDbXref. The location is where participants were recruited, not where the data were analysed or are held. A country the review needs that gaz_xrefs.tsv lacks must be added there first, with its GAZ xref and continent; the script refuses to run otherwise. The script lists the individuals in coho-edit.owl that have no location, so the gaps are visible after each run.

    Participants

    The number of participants a cohort reports is asserted on it by the participants.owl component as numberOfParticipants, with numberOfCases and numberOfControls where its source gives cases and controls separately (and only those where no source states a total). Each is an integer annotation carrying its evidence as annotations on the assertion: the verbatim sentence stating the number (rdfs:comment), the source it came from (oboInOwl:hasDbXref, a PMID or a URL) and what the number counts and the year it refers to (IAO:0000116). The template, src/templates/participants.tsv, is generated by src/scripts/participants.py from src/curation/cohort_participants.csv, the record described above; a row with no number gives nothing, and a quote the check could not find in its source is left off, so the number stands with its source and note alone. Definitions never give a size: the number lives here, as the source states it and never computed, and the participants-evidence check requires every number to carry its source. coho-cohorts.csv has the three numbers and their sources as columns.

    Definitions

    Each cohort's textual definition (IAO:0000115) comes from the definitions.owl component, generated by src/scripts/definitions.py from src/curation/definitions_evidence.tsv (one row per cohort: the definition, its source, a supporting quote and whether the quote was found in the source) and src/curation/definitions_curated.tsv (overrides; an empty definition withholds one). The cohorts to define are the individuals declared in coho-edit.owl, so a newly minted cohort is reported as undefined until a row is added for it. Each definition carries its source as IAO:0000119 (the paper's PMID, or the URL of the cohort's own site; a definition drawn from several sources lists them separated by | in the curated file and carries one IAO:0000119 for each) and, as dcterms:contributor, the agents that drafted and checked it: the definitions were written by an AI model from sources it retrieved and checked by another against those sources, and the annotations say so on every one. A definition supplied through the curated file carries only its drafter's annotation, taken from the file's optional drafted by column (for example "Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), 2026-09" for the cohorts minted from the GWAS and PGS Catalog review); where that column is empty, the definition was written by the checking model alone and names it. The curated definitions have no checked by contributor. Those of the catalog review's third, fourth and fifth tiers were checked against the cohorts' sources by independent reviewers before minting, and those of its second tier after minting (the check rewrote the ones it found wanting), as the curated file's note on each says; the rest have not been checked by a second model or person. src/curation/type_changes.tsv lists the cohorts whose type (cohort or cohort aggregation) the drafting suggested changing, and which were applied.

    Aggregation members

    A cohort aggregation is linked to each cohort it combines, and to each aggregation it includes, by hasCohort, from the aggregation_members.owl component generated by src/scripts/aggregation_members.py from src/curation/aggregation_members_evidence.tsv (one row per aggregation and member from a review of every aggregation's membership: the member as its source names it, its COHO ID where it exists or "new", the source, a quote, whether the quote was verified, and per aggregation whether the listed members are cohorts at all — sources that list countries, sites or institutions are marked include = no) and src/curation/aggregation_members_curated.tsv (overrides; a note beginning "remove" drops a membership). Only members that exist in COHO are asserted; the others are the input to minting new cohorts, after which re-running the script links them. Each assertion carries the quote naming the member as rdfs:comment and the source URL as oboInOwl:hasDbXref.

    Adding a component

    1. Create the template, src/templates/your-component-name.tsv (or .csv)
    2. Add the component to the components list in owlmake.yaml, as above
    3. Bring the repository into step with owlmake.yaml: 
      om update-repo
      This adds the component to the imports of the edit file (src/ontology/coho-edit.owl), tells the XML catalog (src/ontology/catalog-v001.xml) where its IRI is to be found, so that Protégé and the build can load it, and creates a placeholder src/ontology/components/your-component-name.owl.
    4. Build it: om make recreate-components

    Removing a component is the same in reverse: take it out of owlmake.yaml, run om update-repo, and delete its file.

    A component can also be derived from SSSOM mappings (use_mappings: true with a list of mappings) or fetched from a remote resource (source: https://example.org/component-source.owl). One that is generated by a process of its own is described as a target, see Build configuration.
